Part 1 Scrum Accountabilities
S1
Product Goal & Backlog Ownership
The PO's three non-delegable accountabilities
0 / 6 ▾

Per the Scrum Guide 2020, the Product Owner is accountable for maximising product value. These items are non-delegable — the PO may get help, but they own the outcome.

  • I have defined and communicated a clear Product Goal — a long-term objective the Scrum Team plans against, expressed as a meaningful future state of the product. MUST
  • The Product Backlog is the single source of planned work — nothing enters the Sprint Backlog that isn't first in the Product Backlog. MUST
  • I regularly order the backlog based on value, risk, cost, and dependencies — not stakeholder pressure or recency. MUST
  • Backlog items near the top are clear, estimated, and understood by the Developers — ready to enter Sprint Planning without ambiguity. MUST
  • I actively accept or reject completed stories against their acceptance criteria and the Definition of Done — not after the Sprint Review. MUST
  • I understand and can explain the Definition of Done — and I ensure no incomplete work is presented to stakeholders as Done. MUST
S2
Scrum Events Participation
Active contribution — not passive attendance
0 / 5 ▾

The PO is a full member of the Scrum Team. Presence matters, but contribution is what drives value. Tick each item when you are consistently doing it — not just showing up.

  • Sprint Planning: I present the most valuable backlog items, clarify requirements, and collaborate with the team to craft a meaningful Sprint Goal. I do not assign tasks. EVENT
  • Daily Scrum: I am available to answer questions and unblock the team. I do not run the event or use it as a status report for myself. EVENT
  • Sprint Review: I host the event, present the outcome to stakeholders, and update the backlog based on feedback received. The Review is a feedback loop — not a sign-off ceremony. EVENT
  • Sprint Retrospective: I participate fully — including reflecting on my own effectiveness in clarifying, ordering, and being available to the team. EVENT
  • Backlog Refinement: I lead or co-facilitate ongoing refinement so that Sprint Planning is never blocked by unclear or unready items. EVENT
Reminder Scrum events are not "ceremonies." Use the correct language — Scrum Guide 2020 uses "events" throughout.
S3
Stakeholder & Customer Engagement
The PO as the single voice for the product
0 / 4 ▾

The PO represents the customer's needs to the team — and the team's realities back to stakeholders. This requires active management, not passive reception of requests.

  • I know who my stakeholders are and have mapped them by interest and influence. I engage proactively — not only when they raise issues. MUST
  • I make prioritisation decisions transparent — stakeholders understand why items are ordered as they are, not just what is at the top. MUST
  • I gather feedback directly from customers and users — through interviews, observation, or reviews — not exclusively through intermediaries. MUST
  • I protect the team from mid-Sprint scope changes — directing new requests to the backlog and assessing their priority against the Sprint Goal rather than inserting them immediately. MUST
Part 2 SAFe Responsibilities
F1
ART Backlog & PI Planning
Connecting team work to ART-level strategy
0 / 5 ▾

In SAFe, the PO translates ART-level Features into team-level Stories, and actively contributes to PI Planning to ensure the team's iteration goals are aligned with the PI Objectives.

  • I understand the difference between the ART Backlog and the Team Backlog — Features live in the ART Backlog; Stories live in the Team Backlog. I do not conflate them. SAFe
  • I decompose Features into Stories that are small enough for the team to complete within a single iteration, with clear acceptance criteria. SAFe
  • I actively participate in PI Planning — I understand the vision and roadmap, contribute to iteration planning, help craft PI Objectives, and identify dependencies during the event. SAFe
  • My team's PI Objectives are meaningful commitments — not a copy of the iteration plan. They describe the business outcomes the team intends to achieve in the PI. SAFe
  • I prioritise backlog items using Cost of Delay — balancing urgency, value, and risk-reduction — rather than purely responding to stakeholder requests. SAFe
F2
PO Sync & ART Coordination
Your role across teams, not just within your team
0 / 4 ▾

The PO Sync is the primary touchpoint for aligning POs across the ART. It is not a status meeting — it is a coordination event for surfacing dependencies and sharing feedback that affects multiple teams.

  • I attend and contribute to the PO Sync — bringing cross-team dependencies, blockers, and backlog insights that affect other teams or the ART Backlog. SAFe
  • I collaborate with Product Management to stay aligned with the product vision and roadmap — and I bring ground-level delivery realities back to them proactively. SAFe
  • I identify and surface cross-team dependencies early — in refinement, PI Planning, and the PO Sync — rather than discovering them mid-iteration. SAFe
  • I participate in System Demos — inspecting the integrated ART increment and gathering feedback to inform the next iteration's backlog priorities. SAFe
F3
Customer & Stakeholder Engagement in SAFe
Feedback loops that fuel the value stream
0 / 5 ▾

In SAFe, the PO is responsible for fostering the feedback loops that keep the ART delivering genuine value — not assumed value. This requires continuous, structured engagement beyond the Sprint Review.

  • I connect with customers directly — through empathy interviews, Gemba walks, or user research — at least once per PI. SAFe
  • I validate benefit assumptions with real data — not just delivery completion. I track whether delivered features are actually being used and generating value. SAFe
  • I share customer and stakeholder feedback with the ART — not just with my team. I bring insights to the PO Sync, System Demo, and PI Planning where they can inform ART-level decisions. SAFe
  • I balance competing stakeholder perspectives using customer centricity as the anchor — not seniority or volume of requests. SAFe
  • I apply the Plan-Do-Check-Adjust cycle — feedback from each iteration and PI informs continuous refinement of the product vision, roadmap, and backlog. SAFe
Key principle Value is only real when a customer uses a working solution. Delivery completion is a means — customer outcomes are the measure.
